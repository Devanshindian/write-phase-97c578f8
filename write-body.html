Every section of every article is written by its own separate call to the AI. None of them can see what the others wrote. This page shows what one section is handed, the rules it has to follow, and then every section we produced, next to the brief it was given.

01The one idea

An earlier step planned the article: every heading, every sub-heading, and which researched facts belong under each. This step turns that plan into prose, one section at a time.

✍

One call per section

A 12-section article is 12 separate calls, all running at once. Each one sees only its own section.

🔑

Facts are locked

It can only use the facts it was handed, each with a source link. It may not add one from its own knowledge.

✎

Prose is free

The explanations, the examples and the argument between the facts are its own writing.

The one rule
Every fact must come from a card. Never invent one, not even inside an example. After the section is written, code reads the tags back out of the prose and checks each one against the facts that section was actually given. A tag pointing anywhere else is stripped and counted.

02The five words you need

Everything on this page uses these five terms, and nothing else that needs explaining.

Card — one researched fact with its source link. It has an id like [c412]. The writer may only use facts that arrived as cards.
Section job — a 40 to 80 word brief saying what that section must settle. It is the only instruction the writer gets about content.
Shape — the sub-headings for a section, and which cards belong under each one. Decided before writing, so the writer never invents a heading.
Tag — the [c412] marker left in the prose next to a fact, so its source travels with it into the finished article.
Word target — how long the section should be. Worked out from the article's total length and how much material each section has.
